# Spectra viewer

Spectra's viewer: a React app built with Vite. The `a2ui-spectra` tool in [`../spectra/`](../spectra) copies its build and serves it, with the API and the live stream its README describes.

## The page

The page follows the design canvas "Spectra viewer UI": https://claude.ai/artifact/EiYG8HSHhGhPfDkMaRexQ2. A left rail holds Spectra's mark and name, the Turns section and, at its foot, the Keys button and the theme: System, Light or Dark, remembered in the browser. A context bar shows the agent's name, the mode, whether the page hears Spectra's live stream, and the wire proxy's address with a copy button. The agent's name opens a popover with the agent's URL, every A2A version and A2UI extension its card states, each extension with its catalogs, a basic catalog by its short name, and the mode. Under it, the turn list and the turn view sit side by side as panels, with a divider between them. Every divider can be dragged, and the browser remembers where: the turn list's width, the timeline's width, the step's detail's height when open, and the Preview pane's width beside Structure at each preview width, the same for every turn. The detail opens at its remembered height, at most half the height it shares with the result; as the window is resized, the result takes the change.

- **The selection** is in the page address, as `#/turns/<trace id>`, followed by `/steps/<step id>` while a step is selected, picked or landed on. Changing the turn adds an entry to the browser's history; changing the step, or the viewer's own move, replaces it, so Back walks through the turns viewed, each on the step it was left on. An address whose step is not in its closed turn opens the turn as one with no step does, and drops the step. While the newest turn is selected, a new turn from any host is selected as it opens; selecting an older turn stops this, and selecting the newest again resumes it. A turn sent from the composer is selected as it opens. A page opened with no turn in its address selects the newest.
- **A small window**, below 1,280px wide, has a strip in place of the turn list: one status icon per turn, in the list's order, each selecting its turn, with a red dot on a turn with problems and its input and problem count as its tooltip. The strip's button opens the full list over the turn view; the arrows select as they move through it, and a turn pressed, Enter on the turn reached, Esc, a press outside it or the button again closes it. The page is at least 740px wide, and scrolls sideways below that.
- **The turn list collapses** to the same strip at any width, with the button at the right of its header, remembered in the browser. On a wide window the strip's button expands it back in place, and so does `/`. It slides between its width and the strip's in 200ms, easing out: the list, held at its width so its text never reflows, is clipped to the card's shape as the panel narrows and revealed as it widens, while the strip fades in over it where it lands, or away as it opens. The list stays mounted while collapsed, hidden under the strip, so opening it replays nothing and keeps its search and open groups. It switches at once when the system asks for reduced motion; dragging the divider never collapses it.
- **The keys.** The turn list, the strip and the timeline are each one Tab stop, and the up and down arrows select the turn or the step they land on. In the turn list they land on the group headers too, the right arrow opens a group and the left closes it, or moves from a turn to its header. In the timeline, Home selects the first row and End the last, following a live turn again. `n` and Shift+`n` go to the next and previous problem of the turn, wrapping around; `l` jumps to the latest step; `/` focuses the search, opening the full list on a small window; `?` and the Keys button list the keys. A key of one character does nothing while typing in a field, or with Ctrl, Cmd or Alt held.
- **The first screen**, with no turns, shows the agent's name and the live status, the first example of each of its card's skills as prompts that fill the composer, unless the message box cannot send to the agent, and the wire proxy's address to point a host app at. The composer is focused.
- **The turn list** groups the turns by conversation, newest first. Each group is titled by its conversation's first message and its agent, with its number of turns and problems, and opens and closes; a turn with no conversation is a group of its own. Each row shows the turn's status, its time, its input in brief, its duration and its problem count. The search, which `/` focuses, matches a turn's input, its conversation's title and agent, and a trace or conversation id, whole or by its start. A group that gains the selection opens, even one closed by hand.
- **The turn's header** leads with the turn's input. Beside it are the outcome and the check state: the number of problems, "No problems", or "Not checked" and why, with each check that ran, its protocol version and its result in a tooltip. One line below holds the start, the duration, the A2UI version, the agent, and the trace and conversation ids with copy buttons.
- **The timeline** lists the turn's steps in the order they came: the input, each status update, text part, data part, file part and A2UI message, and the outcome last, each with its own icon. A delivery that carries several steps has a row of its own with the steps under it. Each step with a problem carries a red mark and its count. While the turn is open, the timeline is marked Live and follows its newest step, and the result shows the latest state; picking a step stops following, and Jump to latest resumes it. When a turn the view followed closes, the view lands on its first problem, or on its final state when it has none.
- **The problems** are the findings of the four checks, each on the message its path names; the messages web core's message processor rejects; a failed turn's reason, on the outcome; and, while the turn is in view, what its preview page reports. A finding that names no message is on the outcome too. A render error counts in the header, on the turn's row and in the surface switcher, marks its step and is reached with `n`; "Problems only" keeps every step a render error has been on while the turn is in view. It is not part of the record, nor of where a turn lands.
- **The result** shows the agent's reply text in a strip, then the Preview and Structure panes side by side, each shown or hidden by its toggle in the pane's header, both on by default, with a divider between them; the toggles are remembered in the browser. Both show the selected surface as it stood right after the step selected. Structure is the surface's tree above its data model. The agent's A2UI messages of the conversation's earlier turns, then the turn's own up to the step, go one by one into upstream web core's message processor in its relaxed validation mode, with the v0.9 and v1.0 basic catalogs registered and a stand-in catalog with no component schemas under any other catalog id the messages name. Each tree node opens to its properties; a child id no component has shows as missing; the components no parent reaches are listed under "Not under the root". A surface with a component of a stand-in catalog lists its components in the order they came instead, since its catalog's schemas, which say which properties hold child ids, are not known. The surface switcher lists the surfaces the turn touched, then those it did not change, each with its catalog id and its problems, and the surfaces the turn deleted.
- **The preview** draws the surface with its catalog's own renderer, in a page loaded in an iframe: the config's preview pages in config order, then Spectra's built-in page for the basic catalogs. Every page is loaded at connect and stays loaded, laid over the pane's frame when a surface is routed to it. A page says when it is ready which catalogs it renders; the surface goes to the first page whose claims cover the catalogs its messages name at its protocol version, matched with web core's compatibility rule, a page still loading ahead of it holding the surface until it answers. The page gets every message that names the surface, the conversation's earlier turns' and the turn's own up to the step, and keeps up with a live turn. The frame is Narrow, 384px, or Wide, the pane's width, switched in the header and remembered in the browser, and keeps the page's own background in both themes; the header names the page drawing it. A strip above the frame lists what the page reported. A surface no page claims shows "No preview for catalog" and its id, with a line for each page that has not answered in time, or speaks another version of the protocol; such a page is reloaded every few seconds until it answers. A component that failed to draw is a problem on the step whose message carried it, marking the component's line; a message the page refused, on that message's step; an error web core reported, on the component's step or the outcome. A refused message or a web core error on a step that already has a problem of the viewer's own folds under it as a quiet line, as web core's rejections fold under findings.
- **The step's detail**, in a split under the result that can be resized and collapsed, and collapsed to its title line until a step is selected or the view lands on a problem, shows the step's problems, then what it holds: an A2UI message or a data part as JSON, a text part as text, a file part's name, MIME type and size or URI, each with its part's metadata. It ends with the delivery the step arrived in, folded. A delivery's row shows the delivery as the turn record holds it: its parts in order and its metadata. The Input step shows the host's message, its capabilities and its data model. Every JSON block has a copy button and folds its objects and arrays.
- **The composer**, docked under the turn view, is one line until typing and grows with its text up to six lines; Enter sends, and Shift+Enter starts a new line. It continues the conversation of the turn in view, whichever of its turns is selected, when the message box started it: it names the conversation by its first message, says when the agent is waiting for input, and waits while that conversation's turn is open. A conversation the box did not start in this run, and one whose agent named none, is not continued: sending starts a new conversation, and the composer says so. New conversation starts one from the composer alone, until a turn is selected.
- While the live stream is down, or silent past its heartbeat, the context bar says so; when it comes back, the page reads the listing, the box and the selected turn again.

## Stack

React, with Radix primitives and the resizable panels shadcn/ui wraps, on Tailwind in A2UI Composer's configuration (`components.json`), `@a2ui/web_core` from npm for the surfaces, and the preview protocol's messages from `a2ui-spectra-preview` in [`../../typescript/preview/core/`](../../typescript/preview/core). Inter and JetBrains Mono are bundled from Fontsource, so the page fetches nothing from outside Spectra.

## Layout

- `src/model/`: what the viewer reads from turn records: the timeline of steps, the problems on them, the check state, the turn list's groups and search, the conversation replay into surfaces, bindings and JSON lines as the tree and the JSON blocks show them, the input in brief, the A2UI messages and where findings point, the preview's feed, routing and the problems its page reports, the turn's summary, following the newest step and turn, the timeline's keys and the next problem, the turn list's keys, the page's keys, the page address, and what the agent's popover reads from the card. Each test is beside its source as `*.test.ts`.
- `src/state/`: the page's store, fed by the API and the live stream, what each pane reads from it, with the composer's view tested beside it, the theme, the dividers' remembered positions, the panes' toggles, the preview's width and the turn list's collapse, the small window, the preview pages in their iframes, and the render errors of the turn in view.
- `src/components/`: the rail, the context bar, the turn list, its strip, the turn view's parts, the Preview pane and the list of keys.
- `src/testing/`: reads the A2A capture suite's recordings from the conformance workspace for the tests.

## Commands

From `tools/spectra_viewer/`:

- `yarn build`: builds the preview core, type-checks the app and builds it into `dist/`.
- `yarn test`: builds the preview core, type-checks the tests and runs them under Node's test runner, on the recordings in `../../conformance/test_data/a2a_capture/`.
- `yarn lint` and `yarn lint:fix`: ESLint with the root ESLint preset and React's hooks rules.
- `yarn format` and `yarn format:check`: Prettier, with the root `.prettierignore`.

From the repository root, `yarn build:all`, `yarn test:all`, `yarn lint:all` and `yarn format:check:all` run the same across every workspace.
